This lesson uses clear parts of the supplied transcript to practice literary questions and very practical imperatives.

# Transcript

| French | English | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Il est minuit. | It is midnight. | Fixed time expression. |
| Ta main cherche un peu la mienne. | Your hand reaches for mine. | `la mienne` = mine. |
| Comme si c’était la première fois. | As if it were the first time. | `comme si + imparfait`. |
| Tes yeux glissent sur mes lèvres. | Your eyes glide over my lips. | Poetic image. |
| Que reste-t-il de toi en moi ? | What remains of you in me? | Formal inversion. |
| Si demain s’en va, je me cacherai sous tes draps. | If tomorrow goes away, I will hide under your sheets. | `si + présent → futur`. |
| Et si l’aube nous sépare, on se reverra. | And if dawn separates us, we will see each other again. | `se revoir`. |
| Ne t’en va pas. Reste avec moi. | Don’t go. Stay with me. | Negative and positive imperatives. |

# Grammar

## Formal inversion

`Que reste-t-il...?` is a formal or literary question pattern.

Spoken French often prefers: `Qu’est-ce qui reste...?`

## Imperative with pronominal verbs

- `Ne t’en va pas.` — Don’t go.
- `Reste avec moi.` — Stay with me.

## Comme si + imparfait

`Comme si c’était la première fois` = as if it were the first time.

# Important notes

- `Que reste-t-il...?` is grammatically useful but more formal than everyday speech.
- `Ne t’en va pas` should be learned as a whole phrase.
