# Grammar

## Imperative + object pronoun

In a positive imperative, the object pronoun comes after the verb with a hyphen.

- `Dis-le.` — Say it.
- `Regarde-le.` — Look at it/him.
- `Prends-la.` — Take it/her.

## Penser à vs penser de

`Penser à` means **to think about / have in mind**.

- `Je pense à toi.` — I’m thinking about you.

`Penser de` is used when asking for an opinion.

- `Que penses-tu de ce livre ?` — What do you think of this book?

## Apprendre à + infinitif

- `apprendre à vivre` — to learn to live
- `apprendre à conduire` — to learn to drive

# Important notes

- `Au revoir` is the normal everyday goodbye. `Adieu` is much stronger and can imply a final farewell.
- Do not confuse `penser à quelqu’un` with `Que penses-tu de quelqu’un ?`; the preposition changes the meaning.
- The supplied captions contain several recognition errors in the later verses, so uncertain lines are excluded.
