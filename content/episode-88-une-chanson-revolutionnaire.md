# Overview

Tunisian musician **Emel Mathlouthi** describes how music, truth, and freedom shaped her life. A poet gave her a poem to sing. She could not yet know that the song would become connected with a revolution.

For an A1 learner, use this lesson in three passes:

1. Listen once without reading.
2. Listen again and reveal the English line by line.
3. Read the French aloud and review only five new expressions.

# Grammar

## Present tense for beliefs and general truths

**Je crois en la vérité** is present even inside a past story because Emel presents it as a belief that remains true. The present also appears in **J’aime beaucoup la musique tunisienne**.

## Passé composé for completed events

The passé composé uses an auxiliary and a past participle:

- **J’ai grandi.** — I grew up.
- **Il m’a donné un papier.** — He gave me a paper.
- **J’ai appris la musique.** — I learned music.
- **J’ai chanté.** — I sang.
- **Ils m’ont appelée.** — They called me.

## Imparfait for background and habits

The imperfect describes situations, repeated habits, opinions, and ongoing past conditions:

- **C’était la dictature.**
- **Les artistes étaient libres.**
- **Quand j’étais enfant…**
- **J’aimais découvrir…**
- **Le gouvernement contrôlait…**

Think: **imparfait paints the scene; passé composé moves the story**.

## Plus-que-parfait for an earlier past

**Ben Ali était devenu président** means “Ben Ali had become president.” The event was already complete before the past situation being described.

## Future in the past

Use the imperfect of **aller** plus an infinitive:

- **allait marquer** — was going to mark or would mark
- **allait devenir** — was going to become or would become

## Qui, que, and ce qui

- **qui** is the subject: *les gens qui le critiquaient*
- **que** is the object: *un poème que j’ai écrit*
- **ce qui** means “what” or “that which”: *ce qui ne va pas*

## Object pronouns before the verb

- **Il m’a donné…** — He gave me…
- **Tu le chantes.** — You sing it.
- **Ils m’ont appelée.** — They called me.

Here **le** replaces the masculine noun **le poème**.

## Negation

- **Je ne savais pas.** — I did not know.
- **Personne n’avait le droit.** — Nobody had the right.

Before a vowel, **ne** becomes **n’**.

## Infinitive patterns

- **aimer + infinitive** — to like doing something
- **c’est important de + infinitive** — it is important to do something
- **avoir le droit de + infinitive** — to have the right to do something
- **pouvoir + infinitive** — to be able to do something

## Depuis and duration

**Depuis 20 ans** means “for 20 years.” It connects the beginning of a situation to the reference time.

## Comparatives and gradual change

- **bon → meilleur** — good → better
- **de plus en plus grande** — greater and greater

## Gender and agreement

- **toute seule** — by herself
- **tout seul** — by himself
- **inflation forte** — high inflation
- **musique tunisienne** — Tunisian music

## Subjunctive preview

**J’aimerais que tu le chantes** uses the subjunctive after **j’aimerais que**. At A1, memorize the whole pattern: **J’aimerais que tu…** = “I would like you to…”.

# Important notes

- **Mon ami** has a liaison and sounds approximately like *mon-na-mi*.
- Final consonants are often silent: the final **t** in *bout* and the final **x** in *voix* are not pronounced.
- **Chante** and **chantes** sound the same.
- **Moi, je…** adds emphasis: “as for me, I…”.
- **Pauvre Tunisie** personifies the country and expresses sympathy.
- The source episode uses intermediate French. As an A1 learner, understanding every word on the first listen is not the goal.
